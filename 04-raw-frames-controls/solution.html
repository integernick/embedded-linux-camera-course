Step 2 — formats and the saved control list

  • Expected enumeration: one pixel format 'RG10' (10-bit Bayer RGRG/GBGB) with exactly two sizes, 3840x2160 at 30 fps and 1920x1080 at 60 fps.
  • They come from the two-row imx477_frmfmt[] table at the end of nvidia-oot/drivers/media/i2c/imx477_mode_tbls.h, quoted in section 1.
  • The sizes and the rates are the rows. The fourcc comes from the DT mode properties mode_type, pixel_phase and csi_pixel_bit_depth.

In imx477-ctrls.txt, against the device tree (min_gain_val=16, max_gain_val=356, min_exp_time=13, max_exp_time=683709):

  • gain: min=16 max=357 and exposure: min=13 max=683710. Both maximum values are the DT value +1, the tegracam sentinel for a value left indeterminate by alternating exposure (section 5).
  • frame_rate: min=2000000 max=30000000 has no +1. Only gain and exposure get the sentinel.
  • sensor_mode: min=0 max=2. The maximum equals the number of DT modes, not modes − 1.
  • Keep the file. Lesson 09 runs the same -l against virtcam and compares the two lists.
  • The comparison shows the same four tegracam controls with different ranges, which is evidence for the source of the ranges.

Step 2 — the captures

orin$ v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 -c bypass_mode=0,sensor_mode=0
orin$ v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 --set-fmt-video=width=3840,height=2160,pixelformat=RG10 \
        --stream-mmap --stream-count=5 --stream-to=mode0.raw
orin$ v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 -c sensor_mode=1
orin$ v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 --set-fmt-video=width=1920,height=1080,pixelformat=RG10 \
        --stream-mmap --stream-count=5 --stream-to=mode1.raw

The sizes must be exact, because this path adds no header and no padding:

  • mode0.raw = 3840 × 2160 × 2 B × 5 = 82,944,000 bytes
  • mode1.raw = 1920 × 1080 × 2 B × 5 = 20,736,000 bytes
  • The most common failure is a mode1.raw of 82,944,000 bytes. The sensor_mode=1 write was skipped, or done after the format was set.
  • use_sensor_mode_id = "true" in the IMX477 node means that the sensor_mode control selects the mode, not the requested width and height.
  • camera_common_try_fmt() rewrites a size that does not match back to the size of the current mode, without an error.
  • Confirm with v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 -V before streaming.

Step 3 — what the pictures must show

  • The grey picture at 800% zoom shows the 2×2 checkerboard of the mosaic.
  • The color picture shows the scene with a green cast. That is expected. The green sites read higher than the red and blue sites. Nothing scaled the channels, because the ISP is not on this path (Lesson 16).
  • Under normal indoor light the blue sites read lowest. Tungsten and warm LED light contain little blue.
  • Red-object check: over the red area only one site of each 2×2 cell responds strongly, the R site. The grey picture shows a repeating cell of one bright and three dim sites.
  • The darkest sample is well above 0. The sensor adds a constant black level, so a raw zero is not scene black.

Step 4 — the two loops

The exposure loop is in the lab. The gain loop differs in three places: the fixed exposure before the loop, the name of the control, and the values:

orin$ v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 -c exposure=4000
orin$ for g in 16 64 256; do
          v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 -c gain=$g
          v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 --set-fmt-video=width=1920,height=1080,pixelformat=RG10 \
              --stream-mmap --stream-skip=2 --stream-count=1 --stream-to=gain_$g.raw
          echo "gain $g"; stats gain_$g.raw
      done
  • stats is the function of Step 5 of the lab. It reads one frame as 10-bit samples and prints two values.
  • The scale filter in that function keeps the full range of the samples. Without it, ffmpeg compresses the values, and a sample of 900 is reported as 835.
  • Check on the host: for examples/scene.raw, with -video_size 160x120, the same filter prints a mean of 327.479 and a maximum of 900.

Step 5 — the sweep, worked

The deliverable table with example numbers from a fixed indoor scene:

Setting Value Physical Mean (DN) Maximum (DN)
exposure 1000 1 ms 92.4 (illustrative) 411
exposure 4000 4 ms 341.7 1023
exposure 16000 16 ms 953.1 1023
gain (at 4 ms) 16 1.0x 341.7 1023
gain 64 4.0x 891.6 1023
gain 256 16.0x 1014.8 1023

Answers to the three questions:

  • Linearity: 1 ms to 4 ms multiplies the mean by about 3.7, not by 4.0. With the black-level pedestal subtracted from both means, the ratio is about 4.
  • Exposure scales the photon signal, not the pedestal. The same holds for the first gain step before clipping.
  • Flattening: samples clip at 1023, the 10-bit maximum. A maximum of 1023 shows that some samples clip. Clipped samples add no further DN, so the mean approaches 1023, as rows 3 and 6 show.
  • A sweep that measures linearity keeps the maximum below 1023. Dim the scene or limit the exposure range.
  • Two skipped frames: the control writes program the sensor over I2C, and exposure and gain take effect a frame or two later. The value applies to the integration of a following frame.
  • The first frame of a new stream can still have the previous settings. The third frame is after the transition.

Mechanical verification

  • verify-build-jp7.sh runs inside jetson-build-jp7 with TREE at the pristine r39.2.1 tree and SOLDIR at this directory.
  • It byte-compiles the generator of the raw files and the older viewer, and it runs the self-test of the viewer.
  • It checks that the three raw files of examples/ are what the generator writes, byte for byte. Then it re-runs the od transcripts of section 2.
  • It re-runs the arithmetic transcripts: the CID and size arithmetic, the fourcc and the unit conversions.
  • The five pictures of section 3 are not checked by the battery, because the container has no ffmpeg. make_raw_figures.sh makes them on the host.
  • It greps the course's own virtcam files for the use_sensor_mode_id, mode_type, frmfmt and ctrl_cid_list transcripts.
  • It greps the r39.2.1 tree for the kernel lines that the lesson prints: the RG10 fourcc and the camera control class.
  • It greps the stock IMX477 overlay and driver for the use_sensor_mode_id and mode_type lines and the two frmfmt rows.
  • verify-build-rpi.sh checks the Raspberry Pi parts of the sections against the pinned Raspberry Pi tree. It asserts the mode table, the control IDs and the two side effects.
  • Neither script writes into the trees, and neither depends on another lesson. Both finish in seconds. The course battery (tools/verify.sh) runs both inside the build container.
